# Presentation outline, v2 (after Bryan's feedback)

## What Bryan told us

1. **The problems felt abstract.** He couldn't feel what parents go through, so he wondered "is this really necessary?" The product read as a diary or story tool, not something useful.
2. **The demo lacked a "before".** Without seeing the normal NICU experience, he couldn't judge how much better ours is.
3. **It was long, repetitive and a bit boring.** The care features were hard to follow.

## Why it happened

- **We describe pain points; we never show one happening.** "Parents struggle to make sense of fragmented information" is a summary. Bryan needed the moment itself: 10 a.m. rounds, five doctors, words Dad has never heard, and he walks out with nothing he can repeat to Mom.
- **We lead with feeling (story, stars, sky) before usefulness.** The first thing people learn is that days become stars, so they file it as a diary. The useful part (what happened, what it means, what's next, who's caring for her) arrives late and blurs into the sky.
- **The demo answers questions nobody has asked yet.** Each feature lands with no "before", so it has no contrast and no payoff.
- **We repeat the same beat.** Stars and sky show up in the idea film, the demo, the title cards, "Both", "Every day counts" and the close. There are also many statement slides.

## The fix in one line

**Tell Niko's real NICU journey as it is today, moment by moment, then replay those same moments with our product.** The audience feels each pain first, then sees it solved. Usefulness comes first; the sky is how it feels, not what it is.

## Niko and Mila

- **Niko** is real (our research). His family's journey is the status quo: what actually happens today.
- **Mila** is our design persona, a composite of the families we spoke to. Say so out loud once: *"Mila isn't a real baby. She's built from Niko's story and the other families we heard from."*
- **The bridge:** *"Niko's parents lived this. We designed for the next family."*
- **Why keep them separate:** the product uses synthetic data only (team rule), and a composite lets us show a setback and a non-going-home path without putting it on a real family.
- **Before using Niko's photos and details in a recording, confirm we have the family's written consent** (AGENTS.md rule).

### Section 1: Niko's journey, today (the "before")

Use the real details from our interviews. Where we don't have them, ask the parent we spoke with; don't invent them.

| Moment | What happens today | What it feels like (their words) | Need |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First night** | Dad arrives alone; Mom is in recovery. Machines, alarms, people moving fast. Nobody has time to explain. | *"I didn't have the luxury of having my baby right away and putting him on my chest."* | Connection |
| **Rounds** | Doctors talk through the plan quickly at the bedside, in clinical terms. Parents nod, then look words up on their phone. | *"[Medical terminology] is a new language."* | Comprehension |
| **Shift change** | A new nurse each shift. What a parent is told depends on who they ask. | *"The hospital is like an elaborate game of telephone."* | Comprehension |
| **Mom's first visit** | She's missed days. The only record is what Dad remembers and a whiteboard. | (ask: how did Mom catch up?) | Connection |
| **3 a.m., at home** | Calls the unit to ask how the night went, and waits for whoever's free. | *"In the ICU, time just… doesn't exist. It's a mash of time."* | Awareness |
| **A setback** | A breathing tube goes back on. Nobody says why, or what it means for going home. | *"We tell them everything, but sometimes it's too much, they don't understand."* (nurse) | All three |

End the section on today's toolkit: notebook, phone notes, Google, the whiteboard, calling the desk. *"This is how parents keep track of their baby today."*

### Section 3: the three needs, as real questions

| Parents ask… | Today they… | Need |
|---|---|---|
| "What happened while I was away?" | call the desk, ask whoever's on shift | Connection |
| "What does that word mean?" | Google it, or nod and stay confused | Comprehension |
| "What's next, and when are rounds?" | wait, or miss it | Awareness |
| "Who's caring for her tonight?" | ask at the desk | Awareness |
| "Is she getting better?" | piece it together from memory | All three |

This table is the bridge to the demo: every row comes back.

### Section 6: the demo, as before → after

Cut it to about 2:00. Each beat opens with a one-second callback to the "before" moment, then shows the feature. One feature per beat, said plainly.

1. **First night** → Day 1 screen: her name, how small she is, nothing asked of him. *(Connection)*
2. **Rounds and new words** → "What's on her", one thing at a time, then tap any word and see it on her body. *(Comprehension)*
3. **Mom's first visit** → her day as an arc, and the moment Dad read her a story, noted by Priya, with nothing extra for nurses. *(Connection)*
4. **3 a.m.** → night view: "Next care 3:00 a.m. · Mila is resting". *(Awareness)*
5. **What's next / who's caring** → What's ahead and the care team. *(Awareness)*
6. **Is she getting better?** → her sky, **once**: a week, a day, a first. This is where the sky earns its place, as progress at a glance, not decoration. *(All three)*
7. **Going home / not going home** → 15 seconds each, no more.

**Cut from the current demo:** the birth animation (Niko's cold open replaces it), extra title cards, the "Mila's Sky" hero card, star wipes, and repeated "every day is a star" lines.

## What to cut or merge elsewhere

- **Ideation** (brainstorm, sort, thread): down to one slide, or into "How we got here".
- **The "idea and story layer" film:** cut; the demo now carries it.
- **"Both", "Every day counts", "Close":** one ending, not three.
- **"Fear and joy" and the separate NICU definition:** fold into the cold open.
- **Principles:** merge into the needs table (each row is a principle).

## Making it less boring

- **Every section ends on a turn** (a question, a surprise, a "but"), not a summary.
- **Show, don't state:** one real moment beats three statement slides.
- **One idea per slide, one number per section.**
- **Never say "story" before showing usefulness.** Say what it does first; let the sky be the feeling at the end.
