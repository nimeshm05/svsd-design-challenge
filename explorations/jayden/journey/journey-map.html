Mila's long stay

A very preterm, medically complex baby, three to four months in the NICU, and the family around her. Five stages from her arrival, told two ways: the stay that ends with going home, and the one that doesn't.

Mila

Born at 27 weeks, 880 grams. Breathing help that goes down and back up. A stay of about 14 weeks.

Mom

C-section. First sees her a day later. Pumping, 50 minutes from the hospital.

Dad

First at the bedside. Back at work by week three, visits in the evening.

Before the stay: a single day

Parents tell us the stay is many ups and downs, and so is a single day. This is Mila's day 23, the day the demo shows, moment by moment.

A parent who leaves at night and comes back at 2 p.m. missed all of it. The bedside screen's job is to give that day back: each moment on the day's arc, in the order it happened, in warm words with the clinical term beside them. And at the end of every day, the day becomes a star.

This version is why the product never assumes a happy ending: no countdowns, no going-home dates, setbacks told as part of her story, and a sky that means as much if she doesn't go home.

What this means for the story we tell

Show a real day

Demo day 23, a harder day: a restless night, back on her CPAP mask, Mom arriving before dawn, her own clothes for the first time. Ups and downs in one arc.

Give the day back

The parent who wasn't there sees what happened, in order, in words they can follow, and who was with her.

Keep every day

Every day becomes a star, so the weeks don't blur and progress is still visible after setbacks.

Be right in both endings

Nothing on the screen depends on going home. The same sky holds her life either way.
