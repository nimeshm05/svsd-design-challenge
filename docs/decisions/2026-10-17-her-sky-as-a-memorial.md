# If she doesn't go home: her sky as a record of her life

**Question.** The product turns every day into a star. If a baby dies in the NICU, what should the bedside screen do, and when should her sky become something the family keeps?

**Options considered**
1. Show the end of the stay like any other chapter: her sky, a day count, the people who cared for her.
2. Turn the screen off and show nothing.
3. A quiet screen by default, with her sky as a record of her life that the family chooses to open, at their own pace, and keeps afterwards.

**What we chose.** Option 3.
- During comfort care the screen almost disappears: her star, her name, "Her nurse is here with you". No day count, clock or data. The family can turn the screen off.
- "Her sky" is there as an option, never opened automatically. Her life draws in as stars, and her moments appear beneath it all at once, with nothing to click through (touching one lights its star). It ends on "Mila. Loved, every day." and the people who cared for her, family first.
- We first made the moments a step-through ("Next" after each one). It felt like a slideshow or a form at the worst moment, so it moved to the going-home ending ("Her story"), where retelling her stay moment by moment is a celebration.
- Timing: her nurse offers it alongside the memory-making nurses already do (photos, handprints). It goes home in the memory box (a printed constellation card, and a private copy if wanted). No automatic reminders on anniversaries; a family can ask for it later.

**Why**
- Option 1 felt insensitive in testing: counting days and listing caregivers reads like a report at the worst moment.
- Option 2 throws away the one thing families say they lack afterwards: a record that she lived, and of who she was.
- The family stays in control of whether, when and how fast. The word "memorial" never appears on the screen.
- Nothing on the screen ever assumed a happy ending, so this ending uses the same sky as going home.
