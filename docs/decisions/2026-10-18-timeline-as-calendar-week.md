# Timeline as a calendar week of real dates, with no status states

**Question.** The Timeline tab in "What's ahead" showed today plus the next six days: a wide box for today and a 3 × 2 grid of compact days. We redesigned it as a Notion-style calendar week. Two questions came up. Our schedule data counts days of her stay, not dates, so how does a Sun → Sat week get its dates? And should events carry status states (done, in progress, not started, abandoned)?

**Options considered**
1. Dates: pin her day today (Day 23) to the real date and count every other day from it; keep the data's weekday for today (Tuesday) and place it in the real current week; or show "Day 23" in place of a date number.
2. States: color-coded status dots (done green, in progress purple, not started gray, abandoned red); states worked out from the time of day; or none, just the day's events.

**What we chose.** Today is the schedule's Tuesday (Day 23), placed on the Tuesday of the real current week so the date numbers are real. No status states. Sun → Sat sits in one row of seven equal days. Today has a cream stroke (the screen's accent) and lists every entry (time, name, a tag like "Major event" or "Every 3 hours", any note), with thin lines between rows. The other days list one line per event, and days before today are dimmed. Prev / next arrows and a "Today" button move between weeks. Major events keep their star and open their own page.

**Why**
- Parents live by calendar dates and weekdays, not by day of stay. Real date numbers make the week match the wall calendar on any day we record the demo. Keeping today on Tuesday keeps the weekdays in the schedule and the rest of the story consistent.
- We first tried today's card at twice the width. Equal days read as one calm, even week, and the stroke is enough to find today.
- An "abandoned" state in red reads as failure, and our rule is that a step back is never failure. Marking past care as "done" also turns her day into a checklist. A parent needs to know what's happening and when, not to grade it.
- The "Now" line in today's list still shows where the day stands, without labeling each event.
