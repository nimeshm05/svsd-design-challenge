# A stellar nursery, not a memorial

**Question:** Stars are a beautiful metaphor for a baby's days, but in many cultures "becoming a star in the sky" means a child has died. How do we use stars without that reading?

**Options considered**
1. Avoid stars entirely (plants, rings, tides).
2. Stars, but cold and distant: a classic night sky.
3. Stars that are still *forming*: a stellar nursery, wrapped in warm, lit gas.

**Chose:** 3.

**Why:** A star being born inside a nebula is the one image that reads as birth and growth rather than loss. Every visual rule follows from it: stars sit *inside* warm, lit gas, never alone on black; they brighten and grow over the weeks; hard days are dimmer and cooler but never dark or missing; copper warmth is reserved for life and for the parent (halos when you were there, the current day, a new moment arriving). The galaxy spirals *outward* from day 1, so the newest day is always at the growing edge.
